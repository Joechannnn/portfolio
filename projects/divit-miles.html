Making miles conversion easier to follow.

divit lets members turn rewards from everyday payments into Asia Miles. I designed the in-app journey as the UX/UI Designer, covering account linking, error recovery, conversion and trip goals.

Role

UX/UI Designer: research, product flows, UI, testing

Duration

6 weeks · 3 agile sprints

Scope

Account linking · conversion · recovery

Release

50+ product states · reusable components

The problem

Members needed to understand their balance and exchange terms before converting miles. They also had to distinguish divit miles from converted miles, link the right account and recover from errors. Confusion at any of these steps could lead them to abandon the conversion.

The journey needed to explain the transfer, let members change their choices until confirmation and keep the process inside divit.

The process

Research & discovery

  • Compared American Express, HSBC and Standard Chartered to see how established products explain linking and conversion
  • Used Hotjar heatmaps to identify where existing journeys lost attention or created hesitation
  • Traced the highest-risk moments across sign-up, account linking, amount selection, confirmation and recovery

Strategy

  • Supported activation by showing members the source balance, destination and conversion result throughout the journey
  • Prioritised one contained in-app journey and a clear explanation of the two mile types

Working with Cathay Pacific's marketing team

I worked closely with Cathay Pacific's marketing team on the visual branding, copywriting tone and Asia Miles conversion details. We agreed how the partnership and conversion process should appear to members. I used that guidance across account linking, amount selection, review, confirmation and error recovery, so the screens met the marketing requirements and helped members understand each step.

Design

  • Mapped new-member registration, Asia Miles linking, conversion and failure-recovery states
  • Compared three wireframe directions and selected the structure that could scale across more states
  • Built 50+ product states covering the core journey and edge cases, backed by a reusable component library

Testing & iteration

  • Tested with five participants aged 23–48; average task completion was 6m 32s and satisfaction reached 95%
  • Testing exposed confusion between "divit miles" and "converted miles"; clearer labels and a focused explanation resolved it

The solution

  • In-app conversion keeps Asia Miles account linking and conversion inside divit.
  • A review before confirmation lets members check the source balance, choose an amount and review the converted result.
  • Trip goals give members a destination to work towards and a reason to return to their points balance.

Checking the details before a transfer.

Members can see their balance, destination and converted value throughout the journey and check them before transferring.
